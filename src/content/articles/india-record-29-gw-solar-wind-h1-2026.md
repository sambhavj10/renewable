India installed a record of roughly **29 GW of new solar and wind capacity** in the first six months of 2026 — the strongest half-year in the country's history, according to figures compiled by research firms tracking the market.

Solar did the heavy lifting. Around **26 GW of solar PV** was added between January and June, of which approximately **19 GW was utility-scale** — up about 32% from the same period in 2025. Rooftop solar contributed a further **6.4 GW**, more than doubling year on year on the back of the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy programme.

Wind was the laggard: about **2.9 GW** of new capacity, down roughly 16% from the 3.5 GW installed in H1 2025.

## The milestones stacking up

The record half pushed India's cumulative renewable energy capacity to about **288 GW as of June 2026**. Solar now accounts for roughly 56% of installed renewable capacity, with wind at around 20%, large hydro at 18%, and bio-power and small hydro making up the rest.

A few things stand out in the numbers:

- **Gujarat and Rajasthan dominate.** The two western states together accounted for more than half of H1 solar additions — about 7.6 GW and 6.6 GW respectively.
- **Rooftop is no longer a rounding error.** At 6.4 GW in six months, residential and commercial rooftops delivered nearly a quarter of all solar additions.
- **The pipeline remains deep.** Tendering activity by SECI and state agencies continues at pace, increasingly bundled with storage.

## The road to 500 GW

India's target is 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030. Sustaining ~50–60 GW of annual additions is exactly the run-rate that target requires — and for the first time, the first half of 2026 suggests the market can deliver it. The open questions are familiar: transmission build-out keeping pace with generation, discom offtake, and whether wind can find a second wind.

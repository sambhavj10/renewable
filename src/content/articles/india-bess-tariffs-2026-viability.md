India's battery storage market spent 2025 in a race to the bottom on price. Through 2026, that race has reversed — and the correction may be healthy.

Standalone battery energy storage system (BESS) tariffs hit historic lows late last year, bottoming near **₹1.48 lakh per MW per month** in an Andhra Pradesh auction in December 2025. By mid-2026 the numbers had firmed: Gujarat's **GUVNL** concluded its Phase IX tender for **450 MW/900 MWh** at a lowest discovered tariff of around **₹2.32 lakh per MW per month** in August, supported by viability gap funding.

## Cheap, but bankable?

The 2025 lows set records but raised alarms. Analysts at JMK Research and IEEFA flagged that a large share of the two-hour capacity awarded at rock-bottom tariffs sits in an "at-risk" category — bids so aggressive that, against rising cell costs and financing rates, the projects may struggle to be built or financed. Business Today and others documented the same concern: a record-low tariff is only a good deal if the battery actually gets delivered.

Seen that way, 2026's firmer pricing looks less like a setback and more like the market finding a sustainable floor. GUVNL's ₹2.32 lakh clearing price sits close to the roughly ₹2.3 lakh/MW/month benchmark analysts consider realistic for two-hour systems.

## Different formats, different numbers

Comparisons are tricky because tenders are structured differently. Capacity-based auctions quote **₹/MW/month**, while some four-hour tenders — such as a recent UPPCL award — are priced on **energy delivered**, at around **₹6.45–6.46/kWh**. The headline figures are not directly comparable, which is part of why "record low" claims should be read carefully.

The through-line is that storage is now central to India's grid plans — and the market is maturing past the point where the lowest bid automatically wins the headline.

*This article is based on auction results and analysis reported by pv magazine and Business Today.*

In the salt flats of Kutch, near the India–Pakistan border, the single largest piece of India's energy transition is taking physical shape. The **Khavda Renewable Energy Park** in Gujarat is planned to reach **30 GW by 2029** across roughly **538 square kilometres** of otherwise barren desert — a footprint several times the size of a major city, and enough capacity to rival entire national grids.

**Adani Green Energy (AGEL)**, the park's anchor developer, had installed a cumulative **9.7 GW** at the site as of April 2026 and has kept adding blocks through the year, including a **139 MW** project that began commercial operation on **1 September 2026** and a 292 MW block earlier in the spring.

## Not a one-company show

Khavda is increasingly a shared build-out. **SAEL Industries** commissioned a **1 GWp (around 800 MW AC)** plant at the park in January 2026, and **NTPC Green Energy** is constructing its own **1.25 GW** Khavda-II solar project in phases, having energised an initial 165 MW block in February. The clustering matters: a common site lets developers share land, transmission and construction logistics at a scale no single project could justify.

## Why it matters

Khavda is a test of whether India can deploy renewables at genuinely industrial scale — and of whether the grid can absorb the output. The surrounding challenge is evacuation: moving tens of gigawatts of variable power out of a remote corner of Gujarat requires a transmission build-out running in parallel with the panels and turbines. The park also leans heavily on storage and hybridisation to firm its output.

If Khavda is completed as planned, it will stand as the largest renewable energy installation on the planet — a symbol of the sheer physical ambition behind India's 500 GW target.

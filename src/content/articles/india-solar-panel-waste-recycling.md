A solar panel works for around 25 years, then stops. India began installing solar in earnest more than a decade ago — which means the first big cohort is now ageing toward retirement, and with it comes a problem the industry has largely ignored: **what to do with dead panels**. In 2026, the rulebook started to take shape.

## The new framework

The **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)** issued guidelines for the safe handling, transport and storage of solar panel waste, operating under the **E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022**. Those rules place solar PV modules, panels and cells in a dedicated e-waste category (referred to as **CEEW-14**), formally bringing them into India's e-waste regime.

The requirements are practical and strict:

- **Transport** must be in covered trucks to prevent breakage and contamination.
- **Storage** must be in covered, dry, ventilated spaces on **impervious floors**, so toxins can't leach into soil or groundwater.
- **Disposal** in open areas or landfills is **prohibited**; recyclable waste may go only to **registered recyclers**.
- **Safe-storage obligations** run through roughly **2034-35**, bridging the period while a full recycling ecosystem develops.

## Why it matters

A solar panel is mostly recoverable material — glass, aluminium framing, silicon, and small but valuable quantities of **silver** and other metals — alongside components that are hazardous if dumped. Done right, recycling turns a looming waste stream into a **domestic source of materials**, easing some of the import dependence that dogs Indian solar manufacturing. Done wrong, millions of panels become toxic landfill.

The current rules focus on **safe handling and storage**; binding **recycling targets** specific to solar are still being debated and were not fully in force as of 2026. But the direction is set: India is beginning to treat the end of a panel's life as seriously as its beginning.

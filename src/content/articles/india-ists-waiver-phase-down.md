One quiet policy did more than almost any subsidy to shape where India's renewables got built: the **waiver of inter-state transmission system (ISTS) charges**. By letting solar and wind power travel across state lines for free, it made it viable to build giant projects in sun-rich Rajasthan or windy Gujarat and sell the power to distant states. That era is now ending — on a schedule.

## A stepped sunset

The waiver tapers by **commissioning date**:

- Projects commissioned **by 30 June 2025**: full **100%** waiver, locked in for 25 years.
- By **30 June 2026**: **75%**.
- By **30 June 2027**: **50%**.
- By **30 June 2028**: **25%**.
- After **June 2028**: **no waiver**.

Crucially, projects pairing renewables with **co-located battery storage**, and **pumped hydro**, keep a **full waiver until June 2028** — a deliberate nudge toward storage.

## What it changes

Transmission charges are a real cost, and reinstating them makes power wheeled across the country less competitive against generation built closer to demand. Analysts estimate the phase-out could add roughly **₹0.40–0.50 per unit** to affected tariffs. The ratings agency Crisil has argued the near-term hit is limited, since about three-quarters of the affected capacity is expected to be commissioned by June 2026 and so still qualifies for the 75% band.

The longer-term effect is strategic. Developers are rushing to commission early to capture higher waiver tiers, pairing projects with storage to retain the full waiver, and giving fresh weight to building nearer load centres. The policy rationale is one of fairness — spreading grid costs more evenly — but it also marks the maturing of a sector that no longer needs a blanket subsidy to compete.

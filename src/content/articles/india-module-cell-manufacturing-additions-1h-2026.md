India's solar factories are multiplying — but unevenly. According to **Mercom India Research**, the country added **50.6 GW of solar module** manufacturing capacity in the first half of 2026, alongside just **9.7 GW of solar cell** capacity. The five-to-one ratio captures, in a single data point, the structural imbalance that still defines Indian solar manufacturing.

## The module–cell gap

Modules are the easy part. Assembling cells, glass, encapsulant and frames into a finished panel is comparatively low-tech and quick to scale, which is why India's module capacity has raced past 100 GW. **Cells** — the light-absorbing heart of a panel — require far more capital, cleaner processes and tighter know-how, so capacity has grown much more slowly.

The consequence is a supply chain that can stamp out panels but still imports a large share of the cells inside them, predominantly from China. That dependency is precisely what the government's **ALMM List-II** mandate, which requires domestically made cells in many project categories, is designed to squeeze out over time.

## Closing the gap, slowly

The 1H 2026 additions show the direction of travel: cell capacity is finally being built, just not yet fast enough to match module output. Large new lines — including gigawatt-scale TOPCon cell plants commissioned later in the year — should narrow the ratio, and incentives under the production-linked schemes continue to pull investment toward the upstream end of the chain.

For now, India's manufacturing story remains two-speed. The country is well on its way to module self-sufficiency, but true supply-chain independence depends on the harder, slower work of building cells, wafers and the polysilicon behind them.

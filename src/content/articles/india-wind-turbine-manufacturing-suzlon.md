India's wind sector spent much of the last decade in the doldrums — a shift to auctions crushed margins, orders dried up, and turbine makers struggled. In 2026, the clearest sign the sector has turned is the health of its **manufacturers**, led by **Suzlon**.

## The order book tells the story

Suzlon, India's largest wind turbine maker, rebuilt its **order book to roughly 6 GW** and delivered a **record ~2,456 MW in FY2026**, up sharply from about 1,550 MW the year before. Its revenue jumped by more than half year on year. Rival **Inox Wind** reported an order book of around **4.4 GW**. Collectively, India's annual **turbine manufacturing capacity** has climbed to roughly **24 GW a year**, up from about 10 GW in 2014.

## Bigger machines, better economics

Part of the revival is technological. New platforms — Suzlon's S175 at around 5 MW, and comparable larger turbines from competitors — capture far more energy at India's moderate-wind sites than the machines they replace, improving project returns. That is what makes both new builds and the **repowering** of old sites commercially attractive again.

## The 100 GW backdrop

All of this is aimed at a stretch target: **100 GW of wind by 2030**, up from roughly 58–59 GW installed in mid-2026. Hitting it would require wind additions to accelerate well beyond recent years — and that only happens if the domestic supply chain, from blades to gearboxes to towers, can scale with demand.

A strong order book is not the same as a finished turbine on a hill. Land, transmission and connectivity remain real constraints. But for the first time in years, India's wind manufacturers are building capacity in anticipation of growth rather than cutting it in retreat — a meaningful vote of confidence in the sector's second wind.

*This article is based on company results and reporting by Mercom and GreentechLead; order-book and capacity figures vary by date and source.*

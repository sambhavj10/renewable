Solar's biggest constraint in a crowded country isn't sunlight — it's **land**. Every gigawatt of ground-mounted solar competes with farms, forests and villages. **Agrivoltaics** (agri-PV) offers a way around the trade-off: raise the panels high enough, space them wide enough, and you can **grow crops and generate electricity on the same land**.

## How it works

In an agri-PV system, panels are mounted on tall structures — often around **3.5 metres** — with gaps between rows so enough light and rain reach the crops below, and so tractors and workers can move underneath. Shade-tolerant crops can benefit from the partial cover in hot, dry regions, and the shading reduces soil moisture loss. For the farmer, the land earns **two incomes**: the harvest, plus rent or power revenue from the array.

## Where India stands

India is still early. Research bodies estimate around **50 pilot installations** nationwide, including a long-running site run by **ICAR-CAZRI** in Jodhpur. In 2026, **TERI** released field baseline assessments for sites in Madhya Pradesh and Telangana along with project-development tools, aiming to turn scattered experiments into replicable models. One notable project near Jaipur retrofitted an existing 600 kW solar plant by raising the panels so farming could resume beneath them, under the PM-KUSUM framework with research support.

Crucially, the next phase of **PM-KUSUM** has been discussed as including a dedicated **agrivoltaics component of around 10 GW** — which would move agri-PV from pilot to policy.

## The hurdles

Agri-PV costs more to build than ordinary ground-mount, because of the taller, stronger structures. And it raises thorny questions about **land rights and revenue-sharing** between farmers and developers over 25-year project lives, plus a shortage of crop-yield data across India's many agro-climatic zones.

If those are solved, agrivoltaics could let India keep scaling solar without forcing a choice between power and food.

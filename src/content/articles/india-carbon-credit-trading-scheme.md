Most of India's climate policy works by building things — solar parks, factories, transmission lines. The **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)** works differently: it puts a **price on emissions**, creating a market incentive for heavy industry to clean up. And it is no longer theoretical.

## From design to obligation

Compliance duties under the CCTS have **entered into force**, covering roughly **490 entities** across **seven energy-intensive sectors** for the financial year 2025-26. The first four sectors — **aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali, and pulp and paper** — had their targets notified in late 2025, followed by **petroleum refining, petrochemicals and textiles** in early 2026. Two of India's biggest emitters, **iron and steel** and **fertiliser**, are inside the scheme but had their targets still being finalised.

## How it works

The CCTS is an **intensity-based** system: rather than capping total emissions, it sets targets for emissions *per unit of output* — tonnes of CO₂ per tonne of cement or steel, for example. Companies that beat their target earn tradable **carbon credit certificates**; those that fall short must buy credits to comply. The first compliance cycle runs across **FY2025-26 and FY2026-27**, measured against an **FY2023-24 baseline**, with emissions reported to the Bureau of Energy Efficiency.

A separate, **voluntary offset mechanism** lets projects outside the obligated sectors generate credits — but, importantly, obligated entities generally cannot use those offsets to meet their compliance targets, keeping the two tracks distinct.

## Why it matters for clean energy

A carbon price, even a modest one, changes the maths. It makes energy efficiency, fuel switching and clean power more valuable to exactly the industries that consume the most electricity and emit the most. Over time, a functioning carbon market could become one of the most powerful — and least visible — forces pulling Indian industry toward renewables.

The scheme is young, and its real test is whether the price it sets is high and credible enough to move investment. But the architecture is now live.

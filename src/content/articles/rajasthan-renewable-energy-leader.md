If India's solar boom had a capital, it would be **Rajasthan**. The desert state combines the two things utility-scale solar wants most — the **highest solar irradiation** in the country and vast stretches of **cheap, sparsely populated land** — and it has used them to race to the top of the national rankings.

## The numbers

By 2026, Rajasthan had emerged as India's **largest solar state**, with installed solar capacity of roughly **41.7 GW** according to one state-level analysis. Its total renewable capacity, around **47.75 GW**, runs neck-and-neck with Gujarat, which narrowly leads on *total* renewables. In the first half of 2026, Rajasthan added about **6.6 GW of solar — roughly a quarter of the national total** — second only to Gujarat.

The state is also home to the **Bhadla Solar Park**, at about **2,245 MW** one of the largest solar installations on Earth, built across a stretch of desert so harsh it was once considered almost uninhabitable.

## Beyond the mega-parks

Rajasthan's lead isn't only in giant utility projects. It has become a magnet for **open access** — the route by which companies buy renewable power directly — accounting for a large share of national open access additions as businesses chase its cheap solar. Its grid and transmission build-out, including Green Energy Corridor lines, has had to scramble to keep up with the generation.

## The challenges

Leading has its own problems. Concentrating so much solar in one region strains transmission and makes **curtailment** more likely when lines fill during peak sun. Land acquisition, water for cleaning panels in a desert, and getting power out to distant demand centres are constant pressures.

Still, Rajasthan is the clearest proof of India's solar thesis: put panels where the sun is fiercest and the land is cheapest, and the gigawatts follow.

*This article is based on state-level analyses and the Rajasthan government; capacity figures vary by source and date.*

India's companies are increasingly buying their own clean power rather than waiting for the grid to green itself. According to **Mercom India Research**, the country added nearly **6 GW of solar through the open access route** in the first half of 2026 — up about **42%** from 4 GW a year earlier — with the second quarter contributing a record **3 GW**. Cumulative solar open access capacity reached **36 GW** by the end of June.

Open access lets large commercial and industrial (C&I) consumers source electricity directly from a renewable developer and wheel it across the grid, bypassing the local distribution utility's standard tariff. For energy-hungry factories, data centres and commercial campuses, it has become the fastest route to cheaper, cleaner power.

## A deadline-driven surge

Much of the first-half rush was front-loaded. Developers pulled procurement and commissioning forward to beat the phased reduction of the **interstate transmission charges (ISTS) waiver** and the **ALMM List-II** domestic-sourcing rules — the latter of which was subsequently pushed to the end of 2026, but only after the original deadline had already concentrated activity.

Mercom's managing director, Priya Sanjay, linked the record installations and a large project pipeline to steady C&I demand, while cautioning that rising project costs, transmission constraints, and shifting banking and open access regulations are squeezing project economics.

## Where it's happening

The growth is geographically concentrated. **Rajasthan** led additions in the first quarter with roughly 39% of the total, drawn by abundant land and irradiation, while **Karnataka** remains the cumulative leader with over 23% of installed open access capacity.

The open access boom is a quiet but structurally important shift: it channels private capital directly into renewables and ties corporate decarbonisation goals to real megawatts on the ground.

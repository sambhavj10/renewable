India can assemble battery packs, but it still imports the thing that matters most — the **cell**. To fix that, the government launched the **Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) Production-Linked Incentive scheme**, a **₹18,100 crore** programme to build **50 GWh** of domestic cell manufacturing. Four years in, the scorecard is sobering — and slowly improving.

## A slow start

By mid-2026, only around **1.4 GWh** of ACC capacity had actually been established — a small fraction of the 50 GWh target, and all of it from a single manufacturer, Ola Electric. Against that, about **40 GWh** has been *awarded* to four firms, so the gap is between commitments on paper and lines running on the floor. No incentive payouts had been made as of late 2025, reflecting how little qualifying output exists.

## The giga-scale bet

The most consequential project is **Reliance New Energy's** planned **10 GWh** cell factory at Jamnagar, Gujarat, which the company has said it aims to commission ahead of schedule. Land acquisition for the full 40 GWh of awarded capacity was reported complete in early 2026 — a necessary, if unglamorous, milestone. The government has also re-earmarked a further **10 GWh** specifically for **grid-scale stationary storage**, launching a fresh tender in 2026 as battery demand shifts from EVs toward the power grid.

## Why it's hard — and why it matters

Cell manufacturing is capital-intensive, technology-sensitive and brutally price-competitive against established Chinese and Korean giants. Building it is a years-long slog, not a quick win. But the prize is strategic: with battery storage central to India's grid plans and EV ambitions, depending on imported cells is a vulnerability the country is determined to reduce.

The ACC scheme is behind schedule — but the concrete is finally being poured.

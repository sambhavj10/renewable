Every battery India installs today — in an electric scooter, a car, or a grid storage project — will one day be waste. With EV sales and battery storage both scaling fast, the country is heading toward a **battery mountain**, and how it handles that is both an environmental question and a strategic one.

## The scale of what's coming

By one NITI Aayog projection, India could have roughly **128 GWh of recyclable batteries by 2030**. Against that, formal **lithium-ion recycling capacity** today is modest and the figures are contested — on the order of **60,000 tonnes a year** across the leading players (Lohum at roughly 20,000, Attero around 17,500), with other estimates of 35,000–45,000 tonnes running at only partial utilisation because collection is still immature.

## The companies

Recyclers are investing ahead of the wave. **Attero** announced a large multi-year expansion aiming for hundreds of thousands of tonnes of e-waste and battery processing capacity, and claims a large share of the formal market. **Lohum** is targeting tens of thousands of tonnes of capacity and is building out lithium **refining** — not just shredding batteries but recovering battery-grade metals.

## The rules — and the prize

The **Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022** put the obligation on producers through **extended producer responsibility (EPR)**, with recycling targets for EV and storage batteries rising toward **90%**, and a **recycled-content mandate** reported to start around 5% in 2027-28 and climb to **20% by 2030-31**.

The strategic prize is obvious. India imports almost all its **lithium, cobalt and nickel**. A domestic recycling industry turns yesterday's batteries into tomorrow's raw materials — an "urban mine" that reduces import dependence and the need to dig new ones. Getting collection, economics and standards right is what turns a looming waste problem into a supply-chain advantage.

*This article is based on NITI Aayog projections and public reporting; capacity and target figures vary by source and should be checked against the official CPCB rules.*

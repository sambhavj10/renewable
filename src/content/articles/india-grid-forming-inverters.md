A conventional power grid stays stable partly thanks to physics: the heavy spinning turbines in coal, gas and hydro plants store **inertia** that resists sudden changes in frequency. Replace those spinning machines with solar panels and batteries connected through **inverters**, and that natural shock-absorber disappears. The technology that replaces it is the **grid-forming inverter** — and it is becoming one of the most important, least-discussed pieces of India's transition.

## Following vs forming

Most inverters today are **grid-following (GFL)**: they measure the grid's voltage and frequency and sync to it. That works fine in a "strong" grid dominated by conventional plants. But in a **weak grid** — one with a low short-circuit ratio, increasingly common where lots of renewables cluster far from big thermal plants — grid-following inverters can struggle, oscillate or trip.

**Grid-forming (GFM)** inverters instead **set** their own stable voltage and frequency, behaving like a synchronous source and actively holding the grid up. Simulations by India's grid operator, GRID-INDIA, found grid-forming control markedly outperforms grid-following across a range of disturbances, with the advantage growing as renewable penetration rises.

## From advice to mandate

India is moving quickly from recommendation to rule:

- **GRID-INDIA (2026):** proposed that all new battery storage above **50 MW** have grid-forming capability at the point of connectivity, and that solar charging those batteries carry GFM capability on at least 25% of capacity.
- **CEA draft standards:** would require at least **15% of inverters** on new renewable plants (from July 2027) to be grid-forming, and grid-forming control for **all battery storage** power conversion systems.

With planning outlooks projecting tens of gigawatts of batteries (on the order of 80 GW / 321 GWh) and pumped storage, getting this standard right is what lets India run a grid on inverters without blackouts.

It is invisible to consumers — and foundational to everything else.

*This article is based on GRID-INDIA proposals and CEA draft standards as reported by Mercom and SolarQuarter; final rules remain pending.*

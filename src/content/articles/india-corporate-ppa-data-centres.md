Some of the fastest growth in Indian renewables isn't being driven by government tenders at all — it's being driven by **companies buying their own clean power**. Through corporate power purchase agreements (PPAs), signed directly with developers and wheeled over the grid via open access, large energy users are becoming a market force in their own right.

## Data centres lead the charge

The hungriest new buyers are **data centres**, whose electricity demand is surging with cloud and AI workloads — and whose operators have public clean-energy pledges to keep. Recent deals illustrate the pattern:

- **Google** signed two Indian PPAs, including one for a hybrid project at the **Khavda** park and one with CleanMax for about **125.4 MW** (66 MW of solar in Rajasthan and 59.4 MW of wind in Karnataka), explicitly in service of its **24/7 carbon-free energy** goal.
- **Digital Edge** contracted about **83 MW** of solar (with Hexa Climate) for a Navi Mumbai campus.
- **CleanMax and STT GDC** signed a 21 MWp solar PPA for Chennai data centres, part of a partnership now exceeding 130 MW.

## From "green on paper" to "green all the time"

The sophistication is rising. Buying enough annual solar to *net off* a year's consumption is no longer the gold standard; leaders now want to be matched with clean power **hour by hour** — true 24/7 carbon-free energy. That is pushing demand toward **hybrid solar-wind contracts and co-located storage**, because only a firmer supply can follow a data centre's round-the-clock load.

## Why it matters

Corporate PPAs channel **private capital** straight into renewables, independent of government auction cycles, and tie hard-nosed commercial procurement to decarbonisation. As India positions itself as a global data-centre hub, the appetite of these buyers for firm, clean, cheap power may do as much to shape the grid as any subsidy.

For most of the past decade, wind has been the quiet underachiever of India's energy transition — overshadowed by solar's plunging costs and explosive growth. In 2026, there are signs that the sector is finding its feet again.

According to **JMK Research**, India added about **4.69 GW of wind** in the first nine months of 2026, with roughly **1.76 GW — about 37.5% of the total — coming in the third quarter alone**. That sequential pickup matters: it suggests the pipeline is converting after a sluggish start to the year, a pattern trade analysts at Indian Infrastructure have described as a genuine revival rather than a blip.

## What's pulling wind back

Several forces are working in wind's favour:

- **Hybrid and round-the-clock tenders.** As auctions increasingly bundle solar, wind and storage to deliver firmer power, wind's complementary generation profile — stronger in the evenings and through the monsoon, when solar fades — is back in demand.
- **Bigger, better turbines.** Larger rotors and taller towers have improved capacity factors at India's moderate-wind sites, improving project economics.
- **Policy and repowering.** State-level support and interest in repowering older, under-performing wind farms with modern machines are adding to the order book.

## Keeping perspective

The comeback is real but modest. Wind's roughly 4.69 GW over nine months is a fraction of the **35 GW** of solar added in the same period, and the sector still faces land, transmission and turbine-supply constraints. Wind is unlikely to rival solar on sheer volume.

But a grid leaning ever harder on variable solar needs wind's different rhythm — and for the first time in a while, the turbines are turning in the sector's favour.

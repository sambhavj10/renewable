India's **National Green Hydrogen Mission** has been generous with awards. Turning those awards into actual molecules is proving far harder.

Under the mission's **SIGHT** programme — Strategic Interventions for Green Hydrogen Transition — incentives now cover roughly **3,000 MW of electrolyser manufacturing** and about **862,000 tonnes a year (8.62 lakh tpa) of green hydrogen production**, spread across a growing list of companies. Production tenders have been won by a who's-who of Indian energy — including ACME, Greenko, Reliance, BPCL, Waaree and L&T's green-tech arm — across successive rounds.

## The delivery gap

The problem is commissioning. SECI's terms typically give production facilities **36 months from the letter of award** to reach full capacity, and that clock is still ticking for most projects. As of early 2026, India had commissioned only around **8,000 tonnes per annum** of green hydrogen capacity — a tiny fraction of what has been awarded, and a rounding error against the mission's headline goal of **5 million tonnes by 2030**.

The reasons are familiar to anyone watching first-of-a-kind energy industries: green hydrogen is still expensive relative to the grey hydrogen it must displace; offtake contracts are thin because buyers in refining, fertiliser and steel want firm, affordable supply before committing; and the cost of renewable power and electrolysers, while falling, has not yet made the economics comfortable.

## What to watch

The next two to three years are the test. If the awarded projects convert on schedule, India's commissioned capacity should climb sharply from its current sliver. If offtake and cost barriers persist, the gap between a 5 MMT ambition and real output will stay uncomfortably wide.

For now, the mission's pipeline looks impressive on paper — and the real scorecard is how much of it actually gets built.
